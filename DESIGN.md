# Fizzy MDR design direction

## Identity

Fizzy MDR is a personal editorial blog about typewriters, film photography, and technology. The design is content-first, photograph-led, calm, and readable. It should feel specific to a working personal archive, not like a product landing page.

## Audience

Readers come for long-form articles, practical technical notes, and image-rich object stories. The interface should stay quiet so photographs and writing remain the focal points.

## Visual language

- Use neutral light and dark surfaces so photographs and screenshots keep their natural color.
- Use the Ghost accent color for taxonomy, focus, selected state, and a small number of editorial rules.
- Prefer flat surfaces with fine borders. Use shallow shadow only when a card or media object needs separation from the page.
- Keep controls circular, cards softly rectangular, and taxonomy labels compact so shape communicates function.
- Use local, relevant icons. Lucide supplies consistent outline controls; brand marks retain their official silhouettes.

## Typography

- Atkinson Hyperlegible Next is the reading face because long articles require clear character shapes.
- IBM Plex Sans gives headings and controls a firmer editorial structure without competing with article text.
- Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono is reserved for code so technical material remains legible and visually related to the body face.
- Special Elite is limited to quotations where the typewriter reference supports the site's subject matter.

## Layout and motion

Dial: ENERGY 1 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 1

- Article pages use a calm, narrow reading measure.
- Index and archive pages alternate image-led showcases, horizontal story cards, and compact taxonomy grids according to their content.
- Motion occurs only after direct interaction. Featured posts use manual edge controls and pips, and reduced-motion preferences remove their slide animation.

## Decision record

- Color: neutral surfaces frame mixed photography; green is the publication accent and carries taxonomy or interaction state.
- Layout: a narrow editorial column supports reading, while wider archive compositions make scanning visual subjects easier.
- Spacing: larger gaps separate editorial sections; smaller gaps bind metadata to the story it describes.
- Cards: story and taxonomy cards group a real image, title, and metadata; unrelated content is not forced into cards.
- Icons: every icon names a real action, content type, brand, or featured state.
- Directional glyphs: arrows are reserved for carousel controls, pagination, and the single see-all navigation link.
- Gradients: image overlays protect white text, tag hero gradients identify an image-free archive header, and heading rules mark editorial hierarchy.
- Shadows: media and floating utilities receive shallow separation; ordinary cards, navigation, and controls remain flat.
- Accent: green marks categories, featured state, focus, and selected links rather than decorating every surface.
